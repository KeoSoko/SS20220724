This is a very good analysis.

Based on everything you've learned, I would lock these decisions now:

Decision 1: Owner's Status Is Authoritative

Yes.

For members:

Owner active  -> member active
Owner trial   -> member trial
Owner expired -> member expired
Owner cancelled -> member cancelled

Ignore the member's own subscription row for access decisions.

Otherwise you end up with impossible-to-explain scenarios where the owner is paying but a member is blocked.

Decision 2: Members Inherit Trial Access

Yes.

You want onboarding to be frictionless.

If a company owner creates a workspace and invites three staff members during their trial, those staff members should be able to use the product immediately.

Otherwise you'll create confusion during evaluation.

Decision 3: Disable Auto-Cancel On Invite Acceptance

Immediately.

This is the biggest bug uncovered in the assessment.

Today the flow is effectively:

Join workspace
↓
Cancel own subscription
↓
Still checked individually
↓
Lose access

That's likely why only 5 workspaces have multiple members.

I would remove the auto-cancel completely for Sprint 1.

Not guard it.

Not delay it.

Remove it.

You can always introduce cleanup later.

Decision 4: Add Owner Cancellation Warning

Yes, but not Sprint 1.

It's a UI enhancement.

The backend inheritance is the important part.

You can add the warning when you build the Organization/Billing page.

Decision 5: Leave Exports Alone

Yes.

Exports are already stable.

Do not touch them.

The assessment correctly shows they aren't subscription-gated anyway.

One Additional Decision I'd Add
Decision 6: Add Visibility Before Billing

Before charging per-seat, add a simple dashboard metric:

Workspace Members: 4
Current Cost (if billed per seat): R196/month

Display only.

Don't charge it.

Observe usage for a month or two.

With only 5 multi-user workspaces today, you don't yet know how companies will actually use the feature.

What I Would Implement In Sprint 1

Only these two backend changes:

Change 1

Create:

getEffectiveSubscriptionStatus(userId)

Logic:

Owner?
  -> own subscription

Member?
  -> owner's subscription

Use it only in:

requireSubscription()
checkFeatureAccess()

Leave billingService.getSubscriptionStatus() untouched.

The assessment is absolutely correct about keeping those separate.

Change 2

Delete the auto-cancel block during invite acceptance.

Full stop.

That's enough to satisfy the companies asking for:

"One company account, multiple users."

Without:

schema migrations
Paystack changes
seat billing
organization refactors
role renames

It's the smallest change that creates real customer value.

If Replit implements only those two changes and nothing else, I'd be comfortable shipping it.
